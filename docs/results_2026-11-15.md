## Steering batches A–F

**Condition:** one direction, one layer and one strength k. k is in standard deviations of the
direction's natural spread; 0 means no push, and a minus sign means pushing the opposite way.

Each condition runs two checks on 300 tasks:
- **Uncertainty check** ("manip", ~7 min): SE of no-tool answers.
- **Tool-call check** ("calls", ~16 min): call rate when tools are available.

| Batch | What it runs | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| A | SE^dm (stratified high- minus low-SE mean difference) at layer 29, k = −4…+4 | 16 |
| B | act^dm (positive control) and a random direction (placebo) at layer 29, k = −4…+4 | 32 |
| C | Six other directions at layer 29, k = ±2, ±4: SE ridge probe, need, diff, act probe, SE\|fail, SE^dm_fm | 48 |
| D | Core directions (from A and B) in `current` mode, k = ±2, ±4, call check only | 12 |

| F | The rest of the full grid: 9 directions × 3 layers × all k × both modes | 648 |

### Steering (layer 29, sparse_tool)

Unsteered (k = 0): SE 0.695, call rate 18.2%.

| Direction | SE from k = −4 to +4 | Call rate from k = −4 to +4 | Note |

| SE^dm | 0.741 → 0.700 (flat; wrong sign at −4) | 13.5% → 19.0% | Fails the uncertainty check |

\* At k ≥ +2, act^dm's no-tool answers break down (84% unparseable at +4), so this SE drop is
an artefact.

### Patching (sparse_tool, 300 pairs)

Each pair is a high-SE and a low-SE prompt with the same failure rate, from the same
environment and difficulty level. We copy the internal states from one prompt into the other
over the shared 63-token suffix. The readout is the change in tool-use log-odds (TUL), in nats.

- **Full copy, high-SE → low-SE:** +0.5 to +0.8 at layers 19–33, peaking at layers 24–25
  (+0.82). The bootstrap interval excludes 0.
- **Full copy, low-SE → high-SE:** about −0.3 at the same layers. The intervals mostly
  include 0.
- **Layers 0–17:** close to zero.
- **1-D copies at layers 23–26:**
  - SE^dm only: +0.18 to +0.24 (intervals exclude 0), about a quarter to a third of the full effect.
  - act^dm only: +0.23 to +0.33, but the intervals include 0.
  - random: about 0.

### Overall reading

Tool calling seems driven by a separate "act" signal and by task difficulty, more than by the
model's own uncertainty. High-SE prompts push that signal slightly in late layers (the patching
result), but moving along the uncertainty direction does not move uncertainty, so we cannot show
calls following uncertainty. Caveat: SE^dm is a weak handle on uncertainty. In E3, its
projection decoded SE at only 0.38.

## Why the SE probes do not control SE

A probe finds a direction that goes along with uncertainty. That does not mean the model uses
that direction to produce uncertainty.

1. **The probe partly reads task type.**
   - The probe decodes SE at 0.80 overall, but surface prompt features already reach 0.62.
   - Within one environment and difficulty level, it drops to 0.42.
   - Pushing on "looks like a hard task" does not change what the model knows about the answer.
2. **SE is a result, not a stored dial.**
   - SE measures disagreement among sampled answers, which comes from the model failing to pin
     down specific facts while it writes the answer.
   - One added vector neither supplies nor removes that knowledge.
3. **We steer only the prompt tokens.**
   - Answers are generated over many later tokens, and the model can re-derive its own state from
     the question.
   - A tool-call decision is made in the first token or two, which may be why the prompt-only push
     works for act^dm and not for SE^dm.
4. **Layer 29 of 36 is late.**
   - Information can be readable there without later computation using it.
   - Patching places the useful signal at layers 19–26.
5. **The effect is near noise.** Random moves SE across 0.675–0.711; SE^dm moves it across
   0.69–0.74.

**The setup works:** act^dm at the same layer and strength takes calls from 14% to 90%. The model
reads act^dm. It does not read SE^dm in a way that changes uncertainty.
